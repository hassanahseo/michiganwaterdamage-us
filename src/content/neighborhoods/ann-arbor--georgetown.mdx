## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Georgetown

Georgetown is a quiet area in southeast Ann Arbor built mainly during the 1960s. The area centers around the Georgetown Country Club grounds and golf course, bounded by Packard Street and King George Boulevard.

Housing mainly consists of brick and frame ranches, two-story colonial homes, and split-level houses with attached garages. Most homes sit on poured concrete foundations or concrete block basements with wood joists supporting the upper rooms.

Many Georgetown homeowners enjoy finished basements with family rooms, home offices, and laundry spaces. Beneath the concrete floor slabs, copper water lines and cast iron or plastic waste pipes tie into city utility networks.

Drinking water comes from the Huron River and deep city wells, managed by the City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities. Rain runoff and domestic sewage flow through separate buried pipes beneath quiet neighborhood streets.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes in Georgetown face distinct basement moisture and plumbing risks tied to 1960s construction and local soils:

- **Footing Drain Sump Pump Demands:** Many Georgetown homes took part in Ann Arbor's footing drain program. Ground water collected around foundation footings routes directly to indoor sump pits, requiring dependable pump operation during storms.
- **Hidden Copper Slab and Wall Leaks:** 1960s copper supply pipes run beneath basement concrete slabs and through interior walls. Decades of mineral exposure can cause slow pin-hole leaks that soak subfloors before detection.
- **Finished Lower-Level Water Damage:** Finished family rooms and guest bedrooms with drywall and carpeting absorb water quickly during unexpected floods. Moisture trapped beneath floor pads leads to rapid mold growth within two days.
- **Sump Pump Overload in Heavy Rain:** Flat yard grading in parts of Georgetown can cause rainwater to pool near foundations. Intense summer storms test sump pump capacity, leading to water backups if pumps fail.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Georgetown Water Emergency

Taking simple steps protects your home and keeps your family safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Find your main water shutoff valve where the water line enters your utility room. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until the water stops.
3. **Move personal items to dry floors.** Carry rugs, cardboard storage boxes, and light furniture upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Georgetown Property Damage

### Why do Georgetown basements need dependable sump pumps?
Many Georgetown homes disconnected foundation footing drains from city sewer mains under city rules. Ground water now pumps through interior sump pits that must run smoothly during heavy rains.

### How do plumbers locate slab leaks under Georgetown basement floors?
Plumbing contractors use acoustic listening tools and thermal cameras to find pipe leaks beneath concrete without tearing up the entire floor.

### What should homeowners do if water pools near foundation walls?
Cleaning gutters, extending downspout pipes at least six feet away from walls, and re-sloping yard dirt prevents water from seeping into Georgetown basements.
