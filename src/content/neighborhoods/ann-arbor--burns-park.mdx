## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Burns Park

Burns Park is an established neighborhood in south-central Ann Arbor, developed mostly between 1905 and 1940. Located near the University of Michigan campus, the area is known for wide sidewalks, beautiful parks, and tall maple and oak trees.

Housing includes two-story Colonial Revivals, Craftsman homes, American Foursquares, and Tudor Revival cottages. Most homes rest on poured concrete or concrete block walls with full basements beneath wood floor joists.

Many Burns Park homeowners have converted their lower levels into finished family rooms, study libraries, or guest suites. Beneath the floors, older copper water lines and cast iron waste pipes connect to municipal utility mains.

Drinking water comes from the Huron River and deep city wells, managed by the City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities. Rain runoff and domestic sewage travel through separate underground utility lines beneath neighborhood avenues.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes in Burns Park face distinct water and plumbing risks tied to mature street trees and finished basements:

- **Tree Root Intrusion in Clay Sewer Pipes:** Tall canopy trees line residential streets throughout Burns Park. Large root systems penetrate joints in older vitrified clay sewer laterals, causing blockages and sewer backups into basement drains.
- **Finished Basement Water Intrusion:** Many homes feature finished basements with carpeting, drywall, and custom built-in shelves. Groundwater seepage through foundation mortar joints can ruin finished rooms before leaks are caught.
- **Sump Pump Failure in Heavy Rainstorms:** High seasonal water tables require dependable sump pump setups. Power outages or pump mechanical failures during severe storms can flood lower living spaces.
- **Aging Supply Pipes and Pin-Hole Leaks:** Homes built in the 1920s and 1930s often contain aging copper supply lines. Mineral corrosion over decades can cause slow pin-hole leaks inside wall cavities, causing hidden mold.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Burns Park Water Emergency

Following simple safety steps protects your home and keeps your household safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near the water meter in your basement utility room. Turn the round handwheel clockwise until the water stops completely.
3. **Move furniture to dry areas.** Carry area rugs, books, and light furnishings upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage cleanup, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Burns Park Property Damage

### Why do sewer pipes back up in Burns Park basements?
Mature maple and oak trees along street lawns push roots into clay sewer pipes. As roots expand, they trap waste and cause sewer water to back up into low basement drains.

### How can homeowners protect finished basements from water leaks?
Installing a dual sump pump system with battery backup, sloping yard soil away from foundation walls, and clearing gutters helps keep cellars dry.

### Can water damage contractors dry wet hardwood floors?
Yes. Restoration teams use specialized wood floor drying mats and commercial dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of hardwood before boards warp.
