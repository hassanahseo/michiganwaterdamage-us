## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Indian Village Detroit

Indian Village is a premier historic neighborhood on Detroit's east side, developed between 1895 and 1930. Located along Burns, Iroquois, and Seminole Streets, the district sits less than one mile north of the Detroit River.

The neighborhood features over 300 large homes. Styles include Georgian, Arts and Crafts, and colonial revival. Houses feature thick stone and brick walls, slate and tile roofs, and wood trim.

Homes in Indian Village have full basements and multi-floor plumbing systems. Original brass pipes, copper roof gutters, and iron drain stacks remain common.

City water comes from the Detroit Water and Sewerage Department (DWSD). Drainage connects to Detroit's combined sewer pipes, which carry roof rain and sewage through east-side mains.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Because Indian Village sits close to the Detroit River, homes face distinct water risks:

- **High River Basin Water Pressure:** Being close to the river keeps ground water high. Wet clay soils press against old stone and brick walls. Moisture seeps through aging mortar joints into basements.
- **Combined Sewer Overflows in Heavy Rain:** East-side sewer mains can fill up during summer downpours. When city sewer pipes get overloaded, pressurized water pushes backward through basement floor drains.
- **Complex Tile and Slate Roof Leaks:** Historic estates feature steep roofs with copper box gutters and downspouts. When gutters clog with leaves or joints crack, rainwater leaks into attic spaces and ceiling plaster.
- **Aging Water Service Connections:** Many historic homes retain original lead or iron pipes connecting to city water mains. Ground settlement and water pressure can cause underground pipe breaks.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During an Indian Village Water Emergency

Taking structured containment steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your primary shutoff valve where the municipal water line enters your basement foundation. Turn the valve handle clockwise until it stops.
3. **Protect historic flooring and woodwork.** Move antique rugs, wood tables, and books away from damp walls or leaking ceilings. Place plastic foil squares under furniture legs.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing support.** For water extraction, sewage cleanup, or mold removal, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Indian Village Property Damage

### Why do Indian Village basements have moisture issues?
Proximity to the Detroit River keeps water tables high. Wet soil presses against old limestone foundations, causing dampness to seep through mortar joints.

### How does high river water affect home drainage?
When Detroit River water levels rise during storms, city sewer pipes can back up. This backup causes water to pool in street drains and basement lines.

### Can water pumps protect historic basements during heavy rains?
Sump pumps and backup battery pumps help remove groundwater before it rises above basement floor levels.
