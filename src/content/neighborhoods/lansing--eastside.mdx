## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in the Eastside

The Eastside is a Lansing neighborhood set between downtown and East Lansing. Bordering Michigan Avenue and Sparrow Hospital, the area offers easy access for hospital staff, state workers, and university students.

Housing consists largely of older Foursquares, Craftsman bungalows, and two-story wood-frame duplexes built between 1910 and 1940. Most homes sit on poured concrete, concrete block, or brick basement walls with wood floor joists and plaster walls.

Due to its spot between the Capitol and campus, the Eastside features many rentals alongside long-term homeowners. Beneath the streets, older clay sewer pipes and copper water lines connect residences to city utility networks.

Drinking water comes from the Lansing Board of Water & Light (BWL) from deep wells. Rain runoff and wastewater travel through city pipes toward the treatment plant.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes in the Eastside face distinct plumbing and basement water risks tied to housing age and rental turnover:

- **Tree Root Intrusion in Clay Sewer Pipes:** Mature maple and elm trees line Eastside streets. Tree roots easily enter joints in older clay sewer pipes, creating stubborn drain clogs and sewer backups into basements.
- **Rental Turnover and Plumbing Strains:** High tenant turnover leads to frequent plumbing issues such as flushed items, grease buildup, and running toilets that go unnoticed until water damage occurs.
- **Frozen Pipes in Drafty Basements:** Older wood-frame homes often have drafty basement edges and crawl spaces. During harsh Michigan winters, water supply lines freeze and burst, flooding basement areas.
- **Spring Snowmelt and Basement Seepage:** Dense soils and flat terrain cause spring snowmelt to pool near foundation walls. Water presses through older concrete block mortar joints, dampening basement storage and carpets.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During an Eastside Water Emergency

Taking simple steps protects your home and keeps your household safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call the Lansing Board of Water & Light or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main domestic water shutoff valve near where the service line enters your basement. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until water flow stops completely.
3. **Move personal items to dry ground.** Carry rugs, cardboard boxes, and light furniture upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Eastside Lansing Property Damage

### Why do sewer pipes back up in Eastside Lansing basements?
Mature street trees push roots into aging clay sewer pipes. As tree roots expand, they trap waste and cause sewer water to back up into basement floor drains.

### How do property owners prevent frozen pipes in older Eastside homes?
Insulating drafty walls, sealing air leaks, and letting taps drip during freeze alerts stops burst pipes.

### Can water damage pros clean up sewage backups safely?
Yes. Restoration contractors use industrial wet vacuums to pump out wastewater, remove dirty materials, and clean surfaces to kill germs.
