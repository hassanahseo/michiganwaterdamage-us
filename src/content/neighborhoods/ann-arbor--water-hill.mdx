## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Water Hill

Water Hill is a unique northwest Ann Arbor area built along rolling hills rising north of Miller Avenue. The area gets its name from natural underground springs and high water tables that flow downhill toward the Huron River.

Housing includes a mix of early 1900s bungalows, compact mid-century ranches, and modern green home rebuilds. Homes sit on sloping lots with stepped foundations made of block, concrete, or old stone.

Many hillside houses feature walk-out basements, terraced yards, and finished lower living rooms. Beneath these homes, copper water pipes and plastic or cast iron drain lines connect to city utility networks.

Drinking water comes from the Huron River and city wells, managed by the City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities. Rain runoff and domestic sewage move through separate buried pipes down the hillside.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Because Water Hill sits on steep slopes with active underground springs, homes face distinct water risks:

- **Natural Springs and High Groundwater:** Underground springs flow year-round beneath Water Hill slopes. During spring thaw and heavy rains, rising water pushes straight against basement walls and floor slabs.
- **Steep Hillside Surface Runoff:** Rainstorm runoff flows fast down Miller Avenue hillsides. If yard slopes or retaining walls fail, runoff washes against exterior walls, finding cracks in older mortar.
- **Continuous Sump Pump Cycling:** Due to active underground water, basement sump pumps in Water Hill run much more often than in flat areas. Pump burnout or power loss can lead to rapid basement flooding.
- **Aging Drain Lines in Older Cottages:** Many 1920s bungalows retain older steel water pipes or clay sewer lines. Shifting hillside soils can crack buried pipes, causing slow hidden water leaks.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Water Hill Water Emergency

Following quick, simple steps protects your hillside home and keeps your family safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near the water meter in your basement or utility closet. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until the water stops.
3. **Elevate lower-level belongings.** Move rugs, cardboard boxes, and wood furniture away from wet walls or damp concrete floors. Put foil squares under furniture feet.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak repair, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Water Hill Property Damage

### Why do homes in Water Hill have chronic basement water seepage?
Water Hill is known for natural underground springs and high water tables. Saturated ground keeps constant moisture pressing against lower foundation walls.

### How can Water Hill homeowners protect against sump pump failure?
Because sump pumps run often here, installing a backup battery pump helps prevent basement floods.

### Does surface water from uphill neighbors affect foundation walls?
Yes. Rain runoff flowing down steep hillside lots can pool against foundation walls if yard swales, French drains, or retaining walls are not properly maintained.
