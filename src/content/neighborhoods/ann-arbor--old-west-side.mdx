## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in the Old West Side

The Old West Side is a designated National Historic District in Ann Arbor, featuring over 900 historic homes built between 1850 and 1920. Founded by German immigrant craft workers, the area showcases Victorian, Italianate, Queen Anne, and Gothic Revival cottages.

Most homes sit on foundations of Michigan fieldstone, early poured concrete, or clay tile blocks. Wood balloon framing and cedar siding rest atop stone walls, with plaster finishes throughout interior rooms.

Because of the area's historic status, exterior work follows strict local preservation rules. Beneath these historic structures, original clay sewer pipes and vintage iron plumbing lines connect to city utility networks.

Municipal water comes from the Huron River and deep city wells, managed by the City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities. Storm runoff and sanitary wastewater move through separate underground utility mains.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes in the Old West Side face unique water risks tied to foundation age and municipal drainage rules:

- **Footing Drain Disconnection Needs:** Many Old West Side homes took part in Ann Arbor's footing drain disconnection program. Rain runoff from foundation footings routes directly to interior sump pumps rather than the city sewer, making sump pump reliability vital.
- **Fieldstone and Mortar Breakdown:** Aging fieldstone cellar walls rely on soft lime mortar. Over time, ground moisture dissolves the mortar, allowing water to seep through walls during heavy spring rains.
- **Aging Clay Sewer Laterals:** Old clay sewer pipes connect older homes to street mains. Shifting soils and mature oak tree roots crack these fragile pipes, causing sudden drain backups.
- **Basement Moisture and Mold:** High water tables near low points on the West Side create chronic basement dampness. Unchecked moisture leads to peeling paint, damp sill plates, and mold on wood joists.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During an Old West Side Water Emergency

Taking simple safety steps protects historic homes and keeps families safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near the water meter where the city line enters your cellar. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until it stops.
3. **Move historic items to dry floors.** Carry area rugs, vintage furniture, and stored goods upstairs. Place squares of foil under heavy wooden legs to prevent floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage cleanup, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Old West Side Property Damage

### Why do Old West Side homes rely heavily on basement sump pumps?
Many properties disconnected footing drains from the city sewer system under municipal rules. Ground water now flows directly to basement sump pits that must pump water out away from the home.

### How do property owners protect historic stone foundations from water?
Grading yard soil away from foundation walls, keeping gutters clear, and installing a backup sump pump keeps water away from stone footings.

### Can wet plaster walls be dried after an indoor pipe break?
Yes. Water drying contractors use drying fans and dehumidifiers to dry historic plaster walls without tearing them down.
