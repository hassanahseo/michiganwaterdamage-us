## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Garfield Park

Garfield Park is a classic south-side Grand Rapids neighborhood developed between 1910 and 1940. The area centers around historic 30-acre Garfield Park, famous for its green spaces, stone pavilions, and walking paths.

Residential streets feature well-built brick and frame bungalows, Dutch Colonials, English Cottages, and American Foursquares. Most homes have full poured concrete or concrete block basements with wood joists and plaster walls.

Streets throughout Garfield Park feature deep tree lawns planted with tall maple and oak trees. Beneath these shaded streets, original vitrified clay sewer laterals and cast iron drain stacks connect to city sewer lines.

City water arrives from Lake Michigan via the Grand Rapids Water System. Storm runoff and home wastewater flow through separate city mains beneath neighborhood streets.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes near Garfield Park face unique water risks tied to mature parkway trees and low-lying ground:

- **Tree Root Intrusion in Clay Sewer Lines:** Large maple trees send root systems deep into the ground. These roots seek water through joints in older vitrified clay sewer laterals, causing cracked pipes and sewer backups.
- **Saturated Soil Near the Park:** Low-lying land around the 30-acre park holds water during early spring snowmelt. High water tables place pressure against basement foundations, leading to floor seepage.
- **Sump Pump Overload During Storms:** Many finished basements rely on mechanical sump pumps to expel ground water. Heavy rainstorms can overwhelm older pumps, leading to standing water on cellar floors.
- **Aging Drain Stacks and Pipe Joints:** Original galvanized water lines and cast iron waste stacks slowly corrode from the inside out. Slow hidden leaks behind plaster walls often create hidden wood rot and mold.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Garfield Park Water Emergency

Following simple safety steps limits property loss and keeps your family safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call Consumers Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main domestic water shutoff where the city pipe enters your basement floor or wall. Turn the round handwheel clockwise until it stops.
3. **Move furnishings to dry areas.** Carry area rugs, cardboard storage, and small furniture upstairs. Place squares of foil under heavy wooden legs to prevent staining.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak repair, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Garfield Park Property Damage

### Why do sewer pipes back up in Garfield Park basements?
Mature maple trees along parkway lawns push roots into clay pipe joints. As roots grow, they catch debris and block household waste from reaching city mains.

### How do property owners stop water seepage near Garfield Park?
Installing a modern sump pump with battery backup, grading dirt away from walls, and clearing gutters helps keep basements dry.

### Does homeowner insurance cover root damage in sewer laterals?
Standard policies often exclude exterior sewer pipe damage unless you have a sewer backup rider. Check with your insurance carrier.
