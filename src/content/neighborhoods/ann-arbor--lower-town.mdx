## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Lower Town

Lower Town is one of Ann Arbor's oldest settlements, located on the north bank of the Huron River along Broadway Street. Laid out in the 1830s, the area grew as a milling center before becoming a riverside neighborhood.

Housing consists of early wood-frame cottages, brick flats, and modern housing built near the university medical campus. Older homes sit on stone or block foundations, while newer buildings feature poured concrete foundations with underground parking.

Due to its spot near campus, Lower Town houses many hospital staff, students, and long-time residents. Beneath the streets, cast iron water mains and sewer lines link homes to city networks.

Drinking water comes from the Huron River and city wells, managed by the City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities. Rain runoff and domestic sewage travel through buried pipes toward regional treatment plants.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Lower Town's spot along the river creates specific water and plumbing concerns for property owners:

- **Huron River Floodplain Risks:** Homes near the Huron River sit in low ground with shallow water tables. During heavy spring rain and river crests, ground water presses against basement walls and floor seams.
- **Constant Sump Pump Work:** Because water tables stay high along the river, basement sump pits fill fast during rains. Sump pump breakdown or power loss can lead to rapid basement flooding.
- **Rental Housing Plumbing Demands:** High tenant turnover in student and hospital housing places heavy wear on plumbing. Grease in drains and toilet leaks often lead to sudden drain clogs and ceiling stains.
- **Damp Foundation Mortar Wear:** Century-old cottages along Pontiac Trail feature soft lime mortar foundations. Constant damp ground weakens mortar, creating musty smells and basement mold.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Lower Town Water Emergency

Taking simple safety steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near where the service line enters your basement. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until water flow stops.
3. **Move personal items to dry ground.** Carry rugs, electronics, and cardboard storage boxes upstairs. Place squares of foil under wooden furniture feet to avoid stains.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Lower Town Property Damage

### Why do homes in Lower Town experience high groundwater seepage?
Lower Town sits directly in the Huron River basin. High water tables keep surrounding soil wet, forcing moisture through basement walls during storms.

### How can property owners prevent basement floods near the river?
Installing a heavy-duty sump pump with a dedicated battery backup unit and checking perimeter drains helps prevent basement flooding.

### Does homeowners insurance cover river flooding in Lower Town?
Standard homeowners policies do not cover river flood events. Protection against rising river water requires a separate flood policy through federal flood insurance.
