## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Roosevelt Park

Roosevelt Park is an old southwest Grand Rapids neighborhood built between 1890 and 1930. The community centers around the Grandville Avenue business strip, known for its rich culture, family-run cafes, and neighborhood markets.

Housing mainly consists of two-story frame homes, duplexes, and multi-unit flats. Most homes rest on stone, hand-laid brick, or early poured concrete basement walls beneath wood floor joists.

Many properties in Roosevelt Park feature original cast iron waste pipes and vintage steel water pipes. These older systems link to city water and sewer pipes beneath paved neighborhood streets and back alleys.

City water arrives from Lake Michigan via the Grand Rapids Water System. Rain runoff and household wastewater travel through city sewer mains that lead to the regional water plant.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Roosevelt Park's low ground near the Grand River and rail lines creates unique drainage and plumbing concerns:

- **Low-Lying Storm Runoff:** The area sits at a lower grade near the river basin. During heavy rainstorms, surface runoff pools along streets and rail beds, testing basement walls and window wells.
- **Sewer Backups in Basement Drains:** Heavy water flow during spring storms can fill neighborhood sewer lines. Without a working check valve, dirty water can push up through basement floor drains.
- **Old Stone and Brick Mortar Weakness:** Constant moisture around older foundations softens vintage mortar joints. Wet soil presses against walls, causing damp cellars, flaking paint, and mold growth.
- **Rusty Steel Water Pipes:** Many early 1900s homes still use galvanized steel water pipes. Interior rust builds up over time, leading to weak water flow, pinhole leaks, or sudden pipe bursts.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Roosevelt Park Water Emergency

Taking quick, simple steps protects your home and keeps your household safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call Consumers Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near the water meter in your basement. Turn the metal handwheel clockwise until the flow stops.
3. **Move furniture to dry floors.** Carry rugs, cardboard boxes, and light items upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak repairs, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Roosevelt Park Property Damage

### Why do basements in Roosevelt Park get water during heavy rains?
Roosevelt Park sits in a low-lying area near the Grand River. High ground water and storm runoff press against century-old stone and brick foundation walls.

### How do I prevent sewer water from backing up in my basement?
Installing a sewer backwater valve on your main waste line blocks dirty street water from flowing back into your basement floor drains.

### Can water damage pros clean up sewage backups safely?
Yes. Restoration contractors use industrial wet vacuums to pump out wastewater, remove soaked drywall, and clean surfaces to kill germs.
