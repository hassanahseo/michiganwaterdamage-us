## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Moores Park

Moores Park is a historic south-side Lansing neighborhood set along a bend in the Grand River. The area centers around historic Moores Park and its 1915 oval swimming pool, featuring river paths and deep tree lawns.

Housing mainly includes early 1900s bungalows, American Foursquares, and English cottages built between 1910 and 1935. Most homes rest on foundations of Michigan fieldstone, hand-laid brick, or poured concrete block cellars beneath wood framing.

Properties along Moores River Drive enjoy scenic water views, with many homes featuring finished basement workshops or walk-out playrooms. Beneath the streets, vintage cast iron plumbing lines and clay sewer pipes link properties to city utility mains.

Municipal drinking water comes from the Lansing Board of Water & Light (BWL) from deep wells. Rain runoff and domestic sewage travel through separate buried city pipes toward the treatment plant.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Because Moores Park curves directly along the Grand River, waterfront properties face specific moisture and foundation concerns:

- **Riverfront Groundwater Pressure:** Properties situated along the river curve experience high seasonal water tables. During spring rains and river crests, water pressure pushes moisture through basement floor joints and foundation walls.
- **Continuous Sump Pump Demands:** Saturated soil along the riverbank requires active basement sump pump operation. Mechanical pump burnout or power loss during heavy storms can flood finished basements within hours.
- **Tree Root Blockages in Clay Pipes:** Mature riverbank willows, maples, and oaks line Moores Park streets. Tree roots easily infiltrate fragile joints in older clay sewer pipes, causing stubborn drain clogs.
- **Basement Dampness and Mold:** Constant moisture around older fieldstone and brick foundations degrades lime mortar over time. Persistent cellar humidity causes musty smells and mold growth on wood floor joists.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Moores Park Water Emergency

Taking simple steps protects your riverfront home and keeps your family safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call the Lansing Board of Water & Light or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near where the service line enters your basement. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until water flow stops completely.
3. **Move personal items to dry ground.** Carry rugs, cardboard boxes, and light furniture upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Moores Park Property Damage

### Why do basements in Moores Park experience high water seepage?
Moores Park sits along a low river curve. High groundwater tables keep surrounding soil wet, forcing moisture through basement floor seams and foundation walls during spring.

### How can Moores Park homeowners prevent sump pump failures?
Because sump pumps run often along the river, installing a heavy-duty dual sump pump with a dedicated battery backup unit is vital.

### Does homeowners insurance cover river flood damage in Moores Park?
Standard property insurance policies do not cover natural river flooding. Protection against rising river water requires a separate federal flood insurance policy.
