## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in the Westside

The Westside is a historic area situated directly west of the Michigan State Capitol in Lansing. Built largely between 1890 and 1940, the community features quiet, tree-shaded streets lined with well-preserved homes.

Housing styles include two-story Victorian homes, Craftsman bungalows, and Colonial homes. Most homes rest on foundations of Michigan fieldstone, hand-laid brick, or early poured concrete walls with wood floor joists and plaster interior walls.

Many homes along Ottawa Street and Allegan Street house state workers and families who appreciate classic charm. Beneath the parkway lawns, older clay sewer lines and copper or steel water pipes tie into city utility systems.

Drinking water comes from the Lansing Board of Water & Light (BWL) from deep wells. Rain runoff and domestic sewage travel through separate buried city pipes toward the treatment plant.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Homes in the Westside face distinct water and plumbing concerns tied to housing age and mature tree cover:

- **Tree Root Intrusion in Old Sewer Pipes:** Thick tree canopies line Westside parkways. Aggressive roots from mature oaks and maples enter joints in older clay sewer pipes, causing stubborn blockages and sewer backups into basement drains.
- **Mortar Breakdown in Stone and Block Cellars:** Century-old stone and concrete block foundations rely on vintage mortar. Ground moisture slowly dissolves the mortar over decades, allowing water to seep through walls during spring downpours.
- **Corroded Steel Water Pipes:** Many homes built before 1940 retain original steel water lines. Rust buildup inside pipes causes poor water pressure, pinhole leaks, and eventual pipe bursts.
- **Spring Snowmelt and Sump Pump Demands:** Heavy winter snowmelt saturates clay soils across the neighborhood. Rising groundwater tables test basement sump pumps and perimeter tiles, risking basement flooding if pumps fail.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Westside Water Emergency

Taking simple steps protects your historic home and keeps your family safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call the Lansing Board of Water & Light or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve near where the service line enters your basement. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until water flow stops completely.
3. **Move personal items to dry ground.** Carry rugs, cardboard boxes, and light furniture upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water removal, sewage extraction, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Westside Lansing Property Damage

### Why do sewer lines back up in Westside Lansing homes?
Large oak and maple trees push roots into aging clay sewer pipes. As tree roots expand, they trap waste and cause sewer water to back up into basement floor drains.

### How do property owners protect historic stone foundations from water?
Keeping gutters clear, sloping yard dirt away from foundation walls, and running a dependable sump pump with battery backup helps keep cellars dry.

### Can water damage contractors dry historic plaster walls?
Yes. Water drying pros use commercial dehumidifiers and drying fans to dry plaster walls without having to tear them down.
