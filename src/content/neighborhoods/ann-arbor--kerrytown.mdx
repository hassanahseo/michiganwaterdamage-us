## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Kerrytown

Kerrytown is one of Ann Arbor's oldest historic areas, laid out in the 1830s next to downtown. The area features a mix of brick market buildings turned into lofts, two-story Victorian homes, and wood-frame cottages.

Most older properties rest on Michigan fieldstone foundations, hand-laid brick walls, or rough-poured concrete cellars. Heavy timber framing and old pine joists support the upper floors, with plaster walls defining interior rooms.

Many properties have seen updates to house student living, retail shops, and multi-tenant rental flats. Beneath the floors, vintage cast iron drain stacks and older copper supply lines tie directly into city water mains.

Drinking water comes from the Huron River and deep city wells. The City of Ann Arbor Water Utilities manages the supply. Rain runoff and domestic sewage flow through separate buried pipes beneath brick and paved streets.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Because Kerrytown sits along the historic Allen Creek drainage basin near the Huron River, properties face distinct water risks:

- **Allen Creek Drainage Inflow:** The buried Allen Creek storm corridor carries heavy runoff through Kerrytown. During summer storms, rising water in the creek valley puts pressure on stone cellar walls.
- **Fieldstone Foundation Seepage:** Old fieldstone foundations and soft lime mortar let groundwater seep into low cellars. Constant dampness softens floor timbers and causes basement mold growth.
- **Heavy Rental Plumbing Strains:** Dense rental housing and busy ground-floor food markets place heavy daily demands on aging drain lines. Food grease and paper waste can cause sudden drain clogs in shared sewer lines.
- **Aging Water Service Connections:** Old lead and steel lines still connect some early homes to street mains. Aging metal lines corrode over time, resulting in pressure drops or pipe bursts.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Kerrytown Water Emergency

Taking structured safety steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main water shutoff valve where the utility pipe enters the cellar wall. Turn the brass valve handle clockwise until water flow stops completely.
3. **Move personal items to dry areas.** Move area rugs, cardboard storage boxes, and furniture away from wet stone walls or damp floors. Place foil squares under heavy wood furniture legs.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing help.** For water extraction, sewage cleanup, or mold cleanup, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Kerrytown Property Damage

### Why do Kerrytown basements experience water seepage after storms?
The neighborhood rests in the natural Allen Creek valley near the Huron River. Saturated ground presses groundwater through historic stone and brick mortar joints.

### How can property owners protect stone foundations in Kerrytown?
Keeping gutters clear, sloping yard dirt away from walls, and operating a sump pump with battery backup limits basement moisture.

### Does the city require permits for emergency plumbing in Kerrytown?
Cleanup and water drying do not require permits. Major pipe replacements or water heater installs need trade permits from the City of Ann Arbor Building Department.
