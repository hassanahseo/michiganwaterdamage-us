## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Old Town Lansing

Old Town is Lansing's original commercial hub, situated along a bend in the Grand River north of downtown. First settled in the 1840s, the historic area features 1800s brick storefronts, converted warehouses, and wood-frame cottages.

Most older buildings sit on foundations of rough-cut fieldstone, hand-laid brick, or early poured concrete rubble walls. Heavy timber joists and pine subfloors support the upper levels, with plaster walls defining historic interior rooms.

Over recent decades, Old Town has grown into an arts district with galleries, local shops, and second-story lofts. Beneath the streets, cast iron water mains and vintage clay drain pipes tie into city utility networks.

Drinking water comes from the Lansing Board of Water & Light (BWL) from deep city wells. Rain runoff and domestic sewage travel through separate buried pipes toward the city wastewater plant.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Because Old Town sits directly along the Grand River floodway, properties face unique water risks:

- **Grand River Groundwater Pressure:** Sitting on the river bank, Old Town has a shallow water table that rises during spring rains. River water presses against historic stone cellars, pushing moisture through old foundation joints.
- **Damp Rubble Foundations and Mortar Breakdown:** Old fieldstone and brick cellar walls rely on soft mortar. Constant ground moisture softens the mortar, causing crumbling joints and basement mold.
- **Sump Pump Overload During Spring Runoff:** High groundwater along the river basin requires dependable basement sump pump systems. Pump burnout or power loss during heavy storms can lead to rapid cellar flooding.
- **Aging Business and Home Drain Pipes:** Vintage cast iron and clay sewer pipes connect older buildings to street mains. Heavy food service grease and aging pipe joints cause stubborn drain clogs and sewer backups.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During an Old Town Lansing Water Emergency

Taking simple safety steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call the Lansing Board of Water & Light or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main domestic water shutoff valve near where the service line enters your basement. Turn the valve handwheel clockwise until water flow stops completely.
3. **Move personal items to dry ground.** Carry rugs, cardboard storage boxes, and light furniture upstairs. Put aluminum foil squares under wooden furniture legs to avoid floor stains.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Old Town Lansing Property Damage

### Why do basements in Old Town Lansing experience chronic dampness?
Old Town sits directly along the Grand River floodway. High water tables keep surrounding soil wet, forcing groundwater through historic stone and brick foundation joints.

### How do property owners protect historic stone foundations from river seepage?
Running a heavy-duty sump pump with battery backup, fixing soft mortar, and clearing gutters helps keep cellars dry.

### Does standard business or home insurance cover Grand River flooding?
Standard property insurance policies do not cover river flood events. Damage from rising river water needs a separate policy through federal flood insurance.
