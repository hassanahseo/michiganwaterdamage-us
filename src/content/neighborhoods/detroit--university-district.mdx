## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in University District Detroit

The University District is an older neighborhood in north Detroit, built during the 1920s and 1930s. Located near the University of Detroit Mercy, the area features over 1,200 brick homes.

Styles include English Tudor, Georgian, and Spanish Colonial homes. Houses feature brick walls, stone porches, slate roofs, and glass windows.

Homes have full basements, copper supply pipes, and iron drain lines. Many homeowners have preserved oak woodwork, finished basement rec rooms, and plaster walls.

City water comes from the Detroit Water and Sewerage Department (DWSD). Drainage connects to Detroit's combined sewer system, which carries street rain and sewage through city mains.

---

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

University District properties face water risks linked to mature trees and older pipes:

- **Tree Roots in Clay Sewer Pipes:** The neighborhood features mature oak, maple, and elm trees along streets and yards. Tree roots search for water and enter old clay sewer pipes. Roots catch waste, causing sudden basement sewer backups.
- **Combined Sewer Overflows in Rain:** During heavy summer downpours, city sewer pipes fill up fast. Sewer water pushes backward through basement floor drains and laundry tubs.
- **Freeze Breaks in Old Water Lines:** Sub-zero cold freezes water pipes where they enter basement walls. Expanding ice splits pipes, causing water leaks when lines thaw.
- **High Ground Water in Clay Soil:** Dense clay soil holds water around home walls after storms. Wet soil creates ground pressure that pushes water through basement walls.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a University District Water Emergency

Taking structured containment steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Locate your main domestic water shutoff valve where the municipal line enters your basement wall. Turn the brass valve handle clockwise until it stops.
3. **Avoid contact with dirty sewer water.** Wastewater backing up from floor drains contains dangerous bacteria and viruses. Keep children and pets away from wet basement areas.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing support.** For water extraction, sewage cleanup, or mold removal, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About University District Property Damage

### Why do tree roots cause sewer clogs in the University District?
Mature yard trees have roots that search for water. Roots enter joints in old clay sewer pipes, catching paper and causing full drain clogs.

### How can homeowners protect finished basements from storm water?
Installing a sump pump with a battery backup system removes groundwater before it rises through basement floors during power outages.

### What causes water pipes to freeze in older brick homes?
Drafts entering unheated crawlspaces or basement corners allow cold winter air to freeze copper supply lines.
