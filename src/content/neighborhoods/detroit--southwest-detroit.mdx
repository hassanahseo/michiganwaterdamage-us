## Housing Profile and Building Architecture in Southwest Detroit

Southwest Detroit is a vibrant community along the Detroit River and Rouge River basins. The area includes neighborhoods like Mexicantown, Springwells, and Hubbard-Richard. Many local businesses, restaurants, and family homes line Vernor Highway and Junction Avenue. Most homes were built between 1910 and 1940 for auto factory workers.

Housing includes two-story wood frame flats, brick bungalows, duplexes, and storefront apartments. Most houses have full basements built with concrete blocks, brick walls, or poured concrete.

Because homes were built a century ago, plumbing often mixes old iron pipes with copper lines. Drain pipes use iron stacks connecting to clay sewer pipes under yards.

City water comes from the Detroit Water and Sewerage Department (DWSD). Household drains empty into Detroit's combined sewer system, which carries street rain and sewage in shared pipes.

## Local Water, Sewer, and Moisture Challenges

Southwest Detroit faces water risks due to low-lying ground and river drainage:

- **Combined Sewer Backups During Summer Storms:** Low-lying streets in Southwest Detroit sit near the bottom of regional drainage basins. During big summer storms, rain rushes into sewer mains, filling them up fast. Pressurized sewage pushes backward through basement floor drains and laundry tubs.
- **Street Flooding Along Low Intersections:** When rain falls faster than storm drains can take it, water pools on streets and alleys. Standing water presses against basement windows and foundation walls.
- **Old Iron Pipes and Low Water Pressure:** Century-old galvanized iron pipes build up rust inside. This rust narrows pipe openings, causing sudden pipe leaks and weak water flow.

## 4 Critical Safety Actions During a Southwest Detroit Water Emergency

Taking structured containment steps limits property loss and keeps occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Turn off the main water valve.** Find your main water shutoff valve where the city line enters your basement wall. Turn the brass valve handle clockwise until it stops.
3. **Stay away from dirty floodwater.** Water backing up from sewer drains contains dangerous bacteria and viruses. Keep children and pets away from damp basements.
4. **Call for 24/7 restoration or plumbing support.** For water extraction, sewage cleanup, or mold removal, call **(877) 422-0329**. For emergency plumbing and leak detection, call **(866) 487-0556**.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Southwest Detroit Property Damage

### Why do basements flood during heavy rain in Southwest Detroit?
Low ground allows rain to fill combined sewer pipes fast. Excess sewer water backs up into home basements through floor drains.

### How can homeowners stop sewage from backing into basements?
Installing a backwater valve on the main sewer line stops city wastewater from flowing back into your basement during storms.

### What should I do if water pools outside basement windows?
Keep window wells clean of leaves and dirt. Clear surface drains so standing rain runs away from foundation walls.
