Winter weather brings deep cold snaps across Washtenaw County. Sub-zero temperatures strain older boilers and furnaces. Broken heaters or overloaded cords raise fire risks in rental areas near campus.

## Multi-Unit Conversions and Smoke Migration in Rental Housing

A distinct challenge in older Ann Arbor homes is how apartment conversions alter smoke spread. Historic homes partitioned into separate units have complex hidden spaces.

Plumbing chases, wire conduits, and shared heating ducts connect rental units. During a lower-floor fire, rising smoke enters these wall voids. Smoke travels through hidden spaces and bypasses closed apartment doors.

Soot settles inside wall spaces and attics. Heavy timber framing absorbs bitter smoke gases deep into wood pores. In winter, cold air condenses smoke oils onto lumber surfaces. This leaves an oily film on hidden ceiling joists. Contractors must seal these surfaces with barrier paint to stop odors from returning in summer.

## Essential Post-Fire Safety Rules for Occupants

Entering a damaged home too early puts your family or tenants at risk from weak floors and toxic smoke soot. Observe these primary safety rules:

1. **Do not enter until declared structurally sound.** Wait outside until local fire marshals or building inspectors confirm the property is safe to enter.
2. **Check electrical hazards first.** If the main service box is in a damp basement or has fire-damaged feed lines, stay out. Call DTE Energy to shut off electrical power at the pole.
3. **Keep hands off blackened surfaces.** Body oils from hands drive acidic soot deeper into paint, wallpaper, and wood cabinets.
4. **Leave central blowers powered off.** Running the heating system circulates loose ash and carbon particles throughout unburned rooms.
5. **Contact our 24/7 restoration line.** Dial **(877) 422-0329** to speak with an independent fire restoration contractor serving Ann Arbor and Washtenaw County.

## The Comprehensive Fire Recovery and Smoke Neutralization Method

Local fire recovery contractors use systematic stages to protect structures and salvage home contents:

- **Board-Up and Tarping:** Contractors cover broken windows. They secure burned doors and install roof tarps to keep out rain and pests.
- **Pumping Water and Drying Framing:** Water from fire hoses is pumped out right away. Large dehumidifiers run until framing wood is dry.
- **Soot Cleaning with Dry Sponges:** Contractors use dry rubber sponges. They wipe soot from walls and ceilings without smearing dark marks.
- **Thermal Fogging for Smoke Odor:** Fogging machines spray a fine cleaning mist. This mist penetrates wall framing to break down stubborn smoke molecules.
- **Air Duct Cleaning and Sealing Wood:** Air ducts are vacuumed under negative air pressure. Burned wood studs get seal coats to lock in smoke odors for good.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Ann Arbor Fire Damage

### Why does smoke odor spread so easily in converted student rental houses?
Converted rentals often have interconnected wall chases, dropped ceilings, and shared heating ducts. Smoke gases get trapped in these concealed spaces, slowly off-gassing into rooms unless framing is sealed.

### How do restoration specialists clean soot off historic interior woodwork?
Contractors use soft natural rubber sponges and mild alkaline cleaning washes that lift soot particles without softening or abrading vintage oak, walnut, or pine millwork.

### Can fire-damaged electrical wiring be reconditioned?
Wiring exposed to high heat or submerged in firefighting water must be evaluated by an electrician. Heat damages plastic insulation, which usually requires replacing damaged wire runs back to the junction box.

### What documentation is prepared for damaged building items in rental homes?
Contractors prepare detailed line-item inventories listing structural elements and building fixtures that require replacement versus those that can be restored for property records.
