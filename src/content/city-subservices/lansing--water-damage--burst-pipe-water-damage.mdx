## 5 Critical Safety Actions During a Lansing Pipe Burst

Taking structured containment steps limits property loss and keeps building occupants safe:

1. **Check electrical safety first.** If your breaker panel is in a wet basement, stay out. Never walk through standing water to reach switches. Call Lansing Board of Water & Light (BWL) or an electrician to cut power safely from outside.
2. **Shut off the main domestic water valve immediately.** Locate the main shutoff valve. It sits where city water enters the basement. Turn the brass handle clockwise until the water flow stops completely.
3. **Relieve residual line pressure.** Open lowest-level indoor faucets and laundry sinks. This drains trapped water into sinks away from damaged walls.
4. **Protect furnishings and catch overhead drips.** Place plastic buckets beneath sagging ceiling drywall. Poke a small pinhole in the center of wet drywall blisters to release trapped water safely into buckets.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Lansing Burst Pipes

### Why do water pipes burst during Lansing winter freezes?
Water expands as it turns to solid ice inside pipes. Trapped ice creates immense hydrostatic pressure between the freeze point and closed indoor faucets. This pressure splits pipe walls open.

### Can waterlogged plaster walls in historic Lansing homes be saved?
Historic plaster applied over wood lath can often be saved if dried quickly with commercial dehumidifiers. Contractors use non-invasive moisture meters to verify framing dryness before refinishing.

### How do restoration contractors dry water trapped inside wall cavities?
Contractors make small inspection holes behind baseboards. They attach specialized drying hoses that force warm, dry air into stud cavities. This dries structural timber from the inside.

### Does homeowners insurance pay for burst pipe damage in Lansing?
Standard policies usually cover sudden and accidental water damage caused by burst pipes. Property owners must maintain adequate heat inside the home during freezing weather. Check coverage details with your insurance carrier.
