## Combined Sewer Systems and Detroit Wastewater Backups

Wastewater issues across Detroit connect to older drainage systems and heavy storms. Most Detroit residential neighborhoods were built before 1950. Homes in Boston-Edison, Corktown, and Indian Village feature deep cellars. These basements have stone, brick, or concrete walls.

Detroit relies on a combined sewer system. The Detroit Water and Sewerage Department (DWSD) manages these pipes with the Great Lakes Water Authority. Sanitary sewage and street runoff travel through the same underground pipes. During intense summer thunderstorms, cloudbursts quickly overwhelm city sewer lines.

When city mains fill up, rising pressure forces wastewater backward. Dirty blackwater gushes up through cellar floor drains and sinks. Over 6 inches of rain fell during the June 2021 disaster. Conner Creek and Freud pumping stations lost electrical feeds. Runoff flooded thousands of basements on the east side and Jefferson Chalmers.

Heavy clay soil across Wayne County worsens this problem. Saturated ground cannot absorb sudden rain. Water collects around building perimeters. It puts heavy pressure on foundation walls. Water pushes into basements through floor drains and wall joints.

## Finished Recreation Rooms and Category 3 Blackwater

A major cleanup challenge in older Detroit homes involves finished basements. Lower levels often serve as family recreation rooms, laundry rooms, or home workshops.

Municipal sewage is Category 3 blackwater. It carries dangerous bacteria like E. coli and Salmonella. It also has viruses and chemical waste. When blackwater enters a basement, it contaminates all porous surfaces.

Porous materials absorb dirty water fast. Carpet padding, wood baseboards, and drywall take in bacteria deep within fibers. Surface sprays cannot reach germs trapped inside walls. If dirty materials stay inside, bacteria multiply quickly. They release bad odors into living areas. Complete removal of porous items and deep washing are needed to make the home safe.

## 5 Critical Safety Actions During a Detroit Sewage Backup

Taking swift containment steps protects your family from sewer fumes and harmful bacteria:

1. **Evacuate the basement level immediately.** Keep children and pets upstairs away from standing wastewater pools and toxic sewer gases.
2. **Stay away from flooded electrical panels.** If standing blackwater reaches electrical outlets or basement breaker boxes, stay out of the room. Call DTE Energy or an electrician to cut power from the outside meter.
3. **Turn off the main incoming water valve.** Shut the city water valve where the pipe enters your basement. This stops household plumbing fixtures from adding more water to the drains.
4. **Halt all indoor plumbing usage.** Do not flush toilets, run dishwashers, or take showers. Water drained upstairs will spill directly out of basement floor drains.
5. **Call for 24/7 sewage cleanup help.** Dial **(877) 422-0329** to reach an independent sewage cleanup contractor serving Detroit and Wayne County.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Detroit Sewage Cleanup

### Why do municipal sewers back up into Detroit basements during storms?
Detroit uses a combined sewer system. Rainwater and sanitary waste share the same pipes. Heavy downpours overload city trunk lines. High pressure drives wastewater backward through basement floor drains.

### What health risks are linked to residential sewage backups?
Sewer wastewater is Category 3 blackwater. It contains harmful bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli. It also has parasites and bad fumes. These contaminants require full protective gear and safe disposal methods.

### How do contractors eliminate sewer smells from concrete floors?
Contractors remove dirty porous materials first. They pump out all liquid sewage. Then they scrub bare concrete slabs with EPA-registered washes. Air machines with carbon filters run continuously to capture odor particles.

### Can water heaters and furnaces touched by sewer water be saved?
Heating equipment submerged in wastewater must be checked by a heating specialist. Gas valves, blower motors, and electrical switches usually require replacement. This ensures safe heating operation.
