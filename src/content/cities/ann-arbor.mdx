## Ann Arbor Municipal Profile and Environmental Risk Profile

Ann Arbor has an estimated population of 119,875 residents across Washtenaw County, combining a world-class academic research center with historic residential districts and modern subdivisions. The local residential architectural landscape features a substantial volume of vintage student rental housing constructed between 1900 and 1965, interspersed with post-war single-family residential neighborhoods. Older structures frequently feature poured concrete, concrete block, or cut-stone foundation footings that interact closely with elevated ground moisture.

Potable water processing and distribution are managed directly by the City of Ann Arbor Water Treatment Plant & Utilities, drawing raw water primarily from the Huron River with supplemental groundwater wells. Municipal wastewater and stormwater drainage operate through a municipal separate sewer system that discharges treated effluent to the Huron River. To protect municipal treatment capacity during intense rainstorms, the city administers an active Footing Drain Disconnection (FDD) program designed to remove private foundation drain connections from sanitary mains and redirect subsurface water into dedicated sump pump systems or stormwater conduits.

Hydrological vulnerabilities in Ann Arbor are heavily influenced by the historic Allen Creek watershed corridor and the surrounding Huron River drainage basin. Repetitive localized flash flooding occurs along the low-lying Allen Creek floodplain during severe convective summer rainstorms, when rapid stormwater volumes surge across surface roadways and test residential foundation perimeter seals. In addition, sub-surface hydrostatic pressure elevating water tables beneath rental properties poses continuous challenges for basements situated in low-elevation valley bottoms.

## Frequently Asked Questions for Ann Arbor Property Owners

### Why are sump pumps so critical in Ann Arbor homes?
Due to Ann Arbor's municipal Footing Drain Disconnection (FDD) program, many homes have had their foundation perimeter drains disconnected from the city sanitary sewer system and re-routed to interior sump pump basins. If the sump pump fails or loses electrical power during a heavy rainstorm, groundwater quickly rises around the foundation and floods the basement.

### What should an Ann Arbor landlord do if a student tenant reports mold?
Landlords should immediately inspect the reported area, identify and repair any underlying plumbing or foundation leaks, and engage an independent mold remediation contractor. Documenting moisture levels and completing professional remediation helps landlords satisfy Michigan habitability statutes and city rental inspection requirements.

### How does the Allen Creek watershed affect Ann Arbor basement flooding?
The historic Allen Creek corridor runs beneath central Ann Arbor in underground culverts. During severe convective summer storms, heavy surface runoff can exceed culvert intake capacity, causing street flash flooding and elevating local water tables directly beneath nearby residential basements.

### Can an undetected plumbing leak cause water bills to spike?
Yes. Running toilet fill valves, leaking underground irrigation lines, or hairline fractures in supply piping beneath concrete floor slabs can quietly waste hundreds of gallons of water per day. Independent licensed plumbers use electronic acoustic leak detectors to pinpoint hidden leaks without opening walls unnecessarily.
