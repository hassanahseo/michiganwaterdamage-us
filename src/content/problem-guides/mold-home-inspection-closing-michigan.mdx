## Direct Answer: How to Handle Mold Discovered During a Michigan Home Inspection

Discovering visible mold or elevated fungal spore concentrations on a real estate home inspection report in Michigan does not have to cancel your transaction, but it does require prompt professional remediation, moisture source elimination, and certified post-remediation clearance testing. Mortgage underwriters—particularly for FHA, VA, and conventional loan programs—frequently condition loan approval on certified proof that active microbial growth has been safely removed and the underlying water intrusion has been permanently resolved.

In Michigan's real estate market, mold is most commonly flagged in unconditioned attics due to improper bathroom exhaust venting and roof condensation, or in basements and crawlspaces subjected to seasonal high water tables and foundation wall seepage. Unaddressed mold creates legal disclosure liabilities for sellers and immediate health and financing barriers for buyers.

## Mortgage Lending Requirements: How Mold Impacts Financing

When a home inspector flags suspected microbial growth on an inspection contingency report, mortgage underwriters review the findings through strict health and safety appraisal guidelines:

### 1. FHA Loan Guidelines (HUD Handbook 4000.1)
Federal Housing Administration (FHA) appraisal guidelines mandate that the property must be free of hazards that affect the health and safety of occupants, the structural soundness of the improvements, or the marketability of the property. If an appraiser or home inspector notes visible fungal growth or rotted structural roof decking, the underwriter will issue an official repair condition requiring professional remediation before closing funds are released.

### 2. VA Loan Standards (Minimum Property Requirements)
The Department of Veterans Affairs enforces strict Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs) requiring that a home be structurally sound, weather-tight, and free of persistent dampness or microbial contamination. Attics with mold-stained rafters or damp crawlspaces with standing water require professional remediation and inspection sign-off prior to clear-to-close issuance.

### 3. Conventional Loan Underwriting (Fannie Mae & Freddie Mac)
Conventional lenders generally require the appraiser to verify that the property meets basic habitability standards. While minor cosmetic surface mildew on bathroom caulk is rarely flagged, extensive mold growth spanning more than 10 square feet on basement foundation walls or structural roof sheathing triggers mandatory remediation requirements and re-inspection fees.

## Michigan Seller's Disclosure Act (Act 92 of 1993) Legal Obligations

Under Michigan law (MCL 565.951 through 565.966, known as the **Michigan Seller's Disclosure Act**), sellers of residential 1-to-4 family properties are legally required to complete a formal written disclosure statement reporting known material defects:

- **Section on Water Intrusion & Structural Dampness:** The Michigan statutory disclosure form specifically asks sellers whether they have knowledge of water in the basement or crawlspace, roof leaks, drainage or flooding problems, or structural settling.
- **The Obligation to Disclose Known Mold:** If a prospective buyer's inspection report formally identifies mold on the premises, the seller now possesses actual written knowledge of that environmental defect. Even if the current transaction falls through, the seller is legally obligated to update their Seller's Disclosure Statement to reflect the inspection findings before marketing the home to future prospective buyers.
- **Liability for Non-Disclosure:** Failing to disclose known mold or active water penetration can expose sellers to post-closing fraud claims, rescission of the sale, and liability for the buyer's remediation and legal expenses.

## Common Inspection Locations for Mold in Michigan Homes

Understanding why and where mold proliferates in Michigan housing stock helps buyers and sellers negotiate appropriate repairs:

### 1. Residential Attics (Roof Decking & Framing)
Attic mold is the single most common inspection finding across Michigan homes. During harsh winter months, warm, moisture-laden indoor air escapes into unconditioned attics through unsealed ceiling penetrations (recessed lights, attic hatches, plumbing stacks). If the attic lacks proper soffit and ridge ventilation, or if bathroom exhaust fans terminate directly into the attic instead of exhausting outdoors through the roof, moisture condenses on the cold undersides of plywood roof sheathing, fostering dense black and white mold colonies.

### 2. Basements and Sub-Grade Crawlspaces
Because much of Michigan sits over impermeable clay till with seasonally elevated water tables, basement moisture is endemic:
- **Foundation Wall Efflorescence & Fungal Colonies:** Concrete block and fieldstone walls allow moisture vapor to migrate inward, creating white mineral efflorescence and dark fungal patches on perimeter drywall and bottom plates.
- **Sump Basin Moisture Migration:** Uncovered sump pits or poorly sealed perimeter drain lines elevate lower-level relative humidity above 60%, providing the moisture needed for mold spores to colonize stored cardboard, wooden studs, and carpet backing.

### 3. HVAC Systems and Supply Ductwork
In older homes where ductwork runs through unconditioned crawlspaces or humid basements, condensation can form inside metal plenums. During cooling months, dust accumulation inside duct lines acts as an organic food source, circulating mold spores throughout all occupied living areas.

## The 5-Step Real Estate Mold Resolution Protocol

To satisfy inspection contingency deadlines, protect loan approval, and ensure buyer peace of mind, execute this structured protocol:

### Step 1: Secure an Independent Moisture and Mold Diagnostic Report
Commission an on-site physical inspection by an independent mold assessor. The specialist should document the square footage of affected materials, record relative humidity and moisture levels in structural wood framing, and collect air-o-cell spore trap samples (both indoor and outdoor reference samples) for independent laboratory analysis.

### Step 2: Permanently Repair the Underlying Moisture Source
Mold cannot survive without moisture. Remediation is futile if the underlying source of water ingress remains active:
- Re-route bathroom exhaust fan ducts through roof caps with backdraft dampers.
- Clear blocked attic soffit vents and install continuous ridge venting to balance airflow.
- Seal exterior foundation cracks, regrade soil away from basement walls, and clear perimeter gutter downspouts.
- Repair leaking plumbing supply lines, valves, or drain traps.

### Step 3: Professional Physical Remediation Under Containment
Certified remediation contractors follow ANSI/IICRC S520 Standards for Professional Mold Remediation:
- **Negative Air Pressure Isolation:** Technicians seal doorways and HVAC vents with 6-mil poly containment barriers and operate HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent mold spores from drifting into finished living zones.
- **Physical Removal of Porous Contaminated Materials:** Drywall, fiberglass insulation, and carpet showing visible mold penetration are carefully excised, bagged, and removed.
- **Structural Abrasive Cleaning:** Wooden framing, joists, and roof decking are sanitized using dry ice blasting, HEPA wire-brush sanding, or antimicrobial scrub wash.
- **EPA-Registered Biocide & Antimicrobial Sealant Application:** Bare framing lumber is treated with an EPA-registered fungicidal wash and coated with an antimicrobial white encapsulation sealant to inhibit future spore adhesion.

### Step 4: Independent Post-Remediation Clearance Verification (PRV)
Once remediation and drying are complete, an independent third-party environmental testing technician collects air samples inside the containment area. A passing PRV test confirms that indoor spore levels are equal to or lower than outdoor ambient background levels and that no atypical pathogenic strains (such as *Stachybotrys* or elevated *Aspergillus/Penicillium*) are detected.

### Step 5: Escrow Holdback Agreements for Tight Closings
If the scheduled real estate closing date arrives before remediation work or final clearance testing can be completed, lenders will frequently permit an **escrow holdback agreement**:
- The title company holds 1.5 times the written contractor estimate in escrow from the seller's closing proceeds.
- Funds are released to pay the contractor only after the formal post-remediation clearance report and municipal re-inspection certificates are delivered to the title officer and lender.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Mold and Michigan Home Closings

### Who pays for mold remediation during a real estate transaction?
Payment responsibility is a matter of contractual negotiation between buyer and seller. Typically, buyers request that sellers either hire a certified contractor to complete remediation prior to closing or credit the full estimated cost at the closing table. If the lender mandates remediation as a condition of financing, the seller must generally resolve the issue to finalize the sale.

### How long does professional mold remediation take to complete?
A standard residential mold remediation project—such as treating an attic or a finished basement room—typically requires 2 to 4 business days. This timeframe includes containment setup, physical HEPA cleaning, biocide application, structural drying, and overnight laboratory turnaround for post-remediation clearance air samples.

### Will a real estate lender accept a home without a clearance test?
If a lender has issued a formal underwriter condition regarding mold, they will almost universally require a formal Post-Remediation Clearance Verification (PRV) report from an independent laboratory showing passing indoor spore concentrations before they will authorize closing funding.

### Does surface bleach kill mold on wooden attic framing?
No. Household chlorine bleach is formulated with high water content and is an ineffective disinfectant on porous building materials such as wood and drywall. While bleach may temporarily lighten surface discoloration, the chlorine chemical cannot penetrate porous wood fibers, leaving the fungal hyphae root structures intact to regenerate as soon as humidity returns.

### Can a buyer walk away from a purchase agreement if mold is found?
Yes, provided the purchase contract includes a standard real estate inspection contingency clause and the buyer submits a formal written repair request or cancellation notice within the contractual inspection window specified in the agreement.
